# Validation case: single-phase tap regulators in a SWER chain, batch

Asymmetric power flow from a 22 kV three-phase feeder through a SWER isolating transformer (`ab` → `a`, 22 kV / 12.7 kV, earth electrode of 5 + j0.1 Ω, from-side taps −4..4 × 275 V) into a 20 km SWER line, a SWER line regulator (`a` → `a`, 12.7 kV / 12.7 kV, reversed tap numbering 8..−8 × 0.625 %), a 10 km SWER line and a 240 V distribution transformer with a fixed tap. Regulator 401 keeps the 12.7 kV side of the isolating transformer, measured to true earth through the electrode, in its band; regulator 402 keeps the to side of the line regulator in its band, with the line drop compensation of the 10 km SWER line after it. The line regulator has the second rank and is reached only through an `asym_line`. A batch of two scenarios with `any_valid_tap`: the maximum demand (as in the input) needs taps −2 and 4, and 30 % of it needs taps −1 and 2. The input itself has a valid tap, since a batch first regulates its input.

## Generation

The input (`input.json`), the OpenDSS models (`test.dss` with fixed taps, and `test_regcontrol.dss` with OpenDSS RegControls, which must land on the same taps) and the expected output are generated by `tests/data/generators/reduced_phase_opendss_cases.py`, with OpenDSS (via `opendssdirect.py`) as the reference solver. The generator scans every tap position with fixed taps, chooses `u_set` and `u_band`, and asserts that the expected taps are the only valid ones (or, for a wide band, the lowest or highest valid voltage), with a margin of 10 % of the band. Do not edit the files by hand; change the case definition in the script and regenerate. The modelling choices and the command to run are described at the top of the script.
