# Validation case: SWER short circuits with a fault reactance

IEC 60909 short circuits (maximum voltage scaling) with a complex fault impedance (`x_f` ≠ 0) in the `swer-chain` network of `power_flow/single-phase-transformer`, without loads: a 22 kV three-phase feeder (nodes 1 and 2), a SWER isolating transformer (`ab` → `a`, 22 kV / 12.7 kV, earth electrode of 5 + j0.1 Ω), a 20 km SWER line (nodes 3 and 4) and two SWER distribution transformers (`a` → `a`, 12.7 kV / 240 V) to the 240 V nodes 5 (behind the transformer with a from-side tap at +2.5 %) and 6. It is a batch of three scenarios, all single-phase to ground on phase a:

- at the end of the SWER line (node 4) with `z_f` = 10 + j5 Ω; the fault current returns through the earth electrode of the isolating transformer: 166.378 A (the electrode carries 166.293 A, the phasor sum with the charging current of the SWER line);
- at the 240 V node 6 with `z_f` = 0.05 + j0.02 Ω: 1009.67 A;
- at node 4 with a purely reactive `z_f` = j8 Ω (`r_f` = 0): 173.294 A.

An OpenDSS `Fault` is resistive only, so the fault impedance is a series R + jX `Reactor` from the faulted phase to earth. The generator checks after each solve that the `Reactor` current equals V / (`r_f` + j`x_f`) with the OpenDSS node voltage (to 1e-9 relative), which proves the R/X convention of the reference. PGM and OpenDSS agree to about 3.4e-10 relative, so the case passes with rtol 1e-8 and atol 1e-8 with a margin of more than 10x.

## Generation

The input (`input.json`), the OpenDSS model (`test.dss`) and the expected output are generated by `tests/data/generators/reduced_phase_opendss_cases.py`, with OpenDSS (via `opendssdirect.py`) as the reference solver. Do not edit the files by hand; change the case definition in the script and regenerate. The modelling choices and the command to run are described at the top of the script.
