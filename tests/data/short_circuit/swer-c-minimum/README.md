# Validation case: SWER short circuits with minimum voltage scaling

IEC 60909 short circuits with minimum voltage scaling (`short_circuit_voltage_scaling` = `minimum`) in the `swer-chain` network of `power_flow/single-phase-transformer`, without loads: a 22 kV three-phase feeder (nodes 1 and 2), a SWER isolating transformer (`ab` → `a`, 22 kV / 12.7 kV, earth electrode of 5 + j0.1 Ω), a 20 km SWER line (nodes 3 and 4) and two SWER distribution transformers (`a` → `a`, 12.7 kV / 240 V) to the 240 V nodes 5 (behind the transformer with a from-side tap at +2.5 %) and 6. It is a batch of two scenarios, both bolted single-phase to ground on phase a:

- at the end of the SWER line (node 4): 172.359 A (188.876 A with maximum voltage scaling, see `single-phase-transformer-swer`);
- at the 240 V node 5: 2008.01 A (2206.90 A with maximum voltage scaling).

PGM takes the voltage factor *c* from the `u_rated` of the **source** node, not of the fault location. The source is at 22 kV, so *c* = 1.0 for both faults, also for the fault at 240 V, where IEC 60909 would use *c* = 0.95 (the nominal voltage at the fault location is at most 1000 V). The case pins this behaviour; per-fault-location *c* is an open question of the design (see `docs/advanced_documentation/multi-phase-and-swer-design.md`). As in PGM, the reference scales both the source voltage and the source impedance with *c*. PGM and OpenDSS agree to about 3.4e-10 relative, so the case passes with rtol 1e-8 and atol 1e-8 with a margin of more than 10x.

## Generation

The input (`input.json`), the OpenDSS model (`test.dss`) and the expected output are generated by `tests/data/generators/reduced_phase_opendss_cases.py`, with OpenDSS (via `opendssdirect.py`) as the reference solver. Do not edit the files by hand; change the case definition in the script and regenerate. The modelling choices and the command to run are described at the top of the script.
